What's the Best Gratitude Journal App in 2026?

9 min read · Manifest Guides · Updated: September 2026

A gratitude journal app is a phone app that asks you, once a day, to write down a few things you're glad exist, then keeps that habit alive with reminders and prompts. Which one is best depends on what you want wrapped around that thirty-second act of writing. For a dedicated gratitude journal, Gratitude: Self-Care Journal is the best-rated on the App Store. If you'd rather not install anything, Apple's built-in Journal is quietly excellent. 1. Manifest: Vision Board & 369, for gratitude inside a manifestation practice

Manifest: Vision Board & 369 (by ManifestAI, meaning us) treats gratitude as one pillar of a bigger practice rather than the whole app. The gratitude journal comes with optional AI polish that tightens your entry without putting words in your mouth, and it sits next to guided 369 sessions with repetition counters and grid vision boards. A daily numerology reading rounds it out. If your gratitude habit exists in service of goals you're actively working toward, this is the one built around that link. Honest limitation: it's iPhone-only, it's new enough to have no rating history yet, and after the 3-day trial the full experience needs a subscription. If you want gratitude and nothing else, a dedicated app below will fit better.

2. Gratitude: Self-Care Journal, the dedicated gratitude journal

Gratitude (Hapjoy Technologies) is the category's best-rated dedicated app: 4.9 stars across roughly 45,000 US ratings, and it has been refining the same core since 2018. Entries take photos, prompts arrive when the page feels blank, you can record affirmations in your own voice, and everything stays private on your device. It is polished and pleasant in a way that makes daily use easy. Honest limitation: the journal now shares the app with a vision board and a rotation of quote feeds, so the experience can feel crowded when all you wanted was tonight's three lines.

3. Journal by Apple, the free one already on your iPhone

I think Apple's Journal is where most people should start, and I notice app roundups almost never say so. A built-in free app earns nobody a commission. It surfaces journaling suggestions from your photos and places, logs state of mind into the Health app, and syncs privately over iCloud. At 4.8 stars across 317,441 ratings it is the most-reviewed app on this page. Honest limitation: it is a general reflection tool with no gratitude structure. Nothing teaches you the three-specific-things format, and the suggestions nudge you toward documenting your day rather than thanking it. You bring the method; our gratitude prompts work fine pasted into it.

4. 5 Minute Journal, the fixed morning-and-evening format

5 Minute Journal (Intelligent Change) is the app version of the paper journal that defined the modern format: three things you're grateful for and a daily intention in the morning, highlights and a lesson at night. The structure is the product. For people who freeze in front of open-ended pages it's the strongest scaffolding here. Honest limitation: the structure is also the ceiling. If the fixed format starts to grate, there is nowhere else to go inside the app, and at 4.8 across 17,546 ratings its users skew toward people who already love the printed original.

5. Day One, the full journal with gratitude inside it

Day One (Bloom Built, now part of Automattic) is the serious journaling platform: multiple journals side by side, entries in any medium you like, and gratitude arriving as a ready-made template. Everything is end-to-end encrypted. Its listing claims over 15 million downloads, and 118,031 US ratings at 4.8 back that up. The polish is real. If you want one private archive of your whole life with a gratitude journal as a shelf inside it, nothing else here matches it. Honest limitation: you assemble the gratitude routine yourself from templates and reminders, and the features that make it shine, like cross-device sync, sit in the Premium tier.

6. Gratitude Plus, gratitude as a shared practice

Gratitude Plus (Redwheel Apps) does one thing no other app on this list does: shared gratitude circles, where you and a partner or family member see each other's entries. Knowing someone reads your three lines is a different kind of motivator from a streak counter. It works on plenty of people; the 4.9 average across 18,556 ratings suggests the idea lands. Honest limitation: the social layer is the point. Journal alone and you're left with a thinner app than the dedicated options above, plus a public feed you may never want.

7. Orca, the photo-first gratitude journal

Orca (formerly Happyfeed) swaps writing for capturing: entries are photos and short videos of good moments, resurfaced later by throwback widgets and a "gratitude jar" that replays random memories. During my test week its widget put a photo of my half-burnt Saturday pancakes from August 15 back on my home screen, and that ambush of a good morning did more for my mood than any quote card all month. Honest limitation: photo-first means the writing muscle barely gets used, reflection stays shallow unless you force it, and at 4,057 ratings the community around it is small.

8. 365 Gratitude Journal, for guided prompts and challenges

365 Gratitude (UofHappy) leans into guidance: a different prompt every day and a mood check-in, with a community feed of other people's entries alongside. If a blank page is what killed your last journal, being asked a concrete question daily is a real fix. Honest limitation: the gamified wrapper cuts both ways. Points and challenge mechanics read as encouragement to some people and as homework to others, and with 4,517 ratings it's one of the smaller ecosystems here.

Do gratitude journal apps actually help?

Modestly, and mostly if you're struggling. That's the honest reading of the one randomized controlled trial to test a gratitude app directly: in a 2025 study in JMIR mHealth, 120 university students were randomized, and after three weeks the ones who had started with at least moderate distress improved significantly on the trial's distress measures, the DASS-21 (effect size d = 0.68), while students who began the study feeling fine showed no measurable change at all.

The detail from that trial I keep repeating to people: how often participants opened the app had no relationship to how much they improved. The correlation was 0.01. That is zero with manners. So pick the app whose single daily prompt you'll actually answer, and ignore every leaderboard and confetti animation along the way. Answering the prompt matters. Tallying your opens doesn't.

Zoom out and the picture holds its shape. A 2021 meta-analysis of 27 gratitude-intervention studies covering 3,675 participants found small average effects on symptoms of depression and anxiety; its authors put "Effective Self-help?" in the title with the question mark included. Robert Emmons, the UC Davis psychologist behind much of the underlying research, sums up the positive case: "We've studied more than one thousand people, from ages eight to 80, and found that people who practice gratitude consistently report a host of benefits." The word carrying the load in that sentence is consistently, and a well-placed reminder is the one thing every app on this page genuinely adds to a paper notebook. Frequently asked questions

Is there a completely free gratitude journal app?

Yes: Journal by Apple ships with iOS and has no paid tier at all. Every other app here is free to download with an optional subscription for the full feature set. If price is the whole question, start with Apple's Journal and upgrade to a dedicated app once the habit proves itself.

What is the most popular gratitude journal app?

By ratings volume, Journal by Apple leads with 317,441 US ratings at 4.8 stars as of September 2026, helped enormously by being pre-installed. Among dedicated gratitude apps, Gratitude: Self-Care Journal is the clear leader with about 45,000 ratings at 4.9.

Do gratitude journal apps actually work?

The evidence is modest, and pages that say otherwise are selling harder than the data allows. The 2025 app trial found significant improvement for students with at least moderate distress and none for those already doing fine; the 2021 meta-analysis found small average effects across 27 studies. Treat an app as a consistency tool, never as treatment. It pairs well with the habits covered in our morning routine guide.

What should I write in a gratitude journal app?

Specific beats grand. "Took the stairs and my knees said nothing" outlasts "my health", because an entry you couldn't have written yesterday forces you to notice something new. Three lines a day is plenty, and our gratitude journal prompts cover the blank-page days. If your gratitude practice feeds bigger goals, a manifestation journal structure ties the two together.
